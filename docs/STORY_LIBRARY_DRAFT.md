## The writing approach

- Use short questions, common words and one main ask at a time. Read every question aloud. Simple language should still sound like two adults talking.
- Make the subject concrete: a date, a joke, a small money choice, a tired evening. A question becomes personal through the detail the couple brings, not through a demand to share a secret.
- Stay with the same subject for five questions. Vary the arc: a memory can move through time; a playful story can build a scene; a harder topic can move from an everyday example toward a clearer request.
- Let both people answer. They can remember different things, want different things, skip any question or stop. No right answer, test of memory, forced thanks, promise or required agreement.
- A nudge helps someone get started. It is not another required question. Questions remain understandable when the nudge is closed.
- Each screen names enough of the subject to orient someone who skipped an earlier question. Shared references stay broad; nothing claims that an earlier answer was given or agreed.
- End with an optional invitation. Saving a Moment is never needed to finish. Some stories simply end with a detail or a laugh; they do not all become a task for the week.
- Do not assume marriage, children, gender, shared money, living together, travel budgets, good childhood memories or a desire for touch.

Shared opening copy: **“Take turns if you like. You can skip any question.”**

For Personal or Direct topics, use the short expectation below on the topic preview. Keep the ordinary skip and stop controls available throughout.

## S01 · Before we said hello

**Invitation:** Go back to our first date, from getting ready to saying goodbye.

**Topic fit:** Choose the first meeting that felt like a date to you. It does not need to have had that name at the time. Different memories are welcome.

1. **Before the date** — What was on your mind before our first date?

   *Nudge:* Think of a hope, a worry, or what the rest of your day was like.

2. **Getting ready** — What do you remember doing to get ready for our first date?

   *Nudge:* Picking clothes, checking the time, or coming straight from something else all count.

3. **The first hello** — When you first saw me that day, what caught your eye?

   *Nudge:* A smile, a coat, the way I stood. A rough memory is enough.

4. **One moment** — What small moment from our first date has stayed with you?

   *Nudge:* It can be sweet, awkward, funny, or just clear in your mind.

5. **Saying goodbye** — When our first date ended, what did you hope would happen next?

   *Nudge:* You may have felt sure, unsure, or ready to see where it went.

**Closing invitation:** If you like, keep one detail from the start of our story.

## S02 · That is so us

**Invitation:** Bring back a moment that still makes us laugh.

**Topic fit:** Pick a memory you both feel okay laughing about. If no shared funny moment comes to mind, choose another story.

1. **The memory** — What is one moment with me that still makes you laugh?

   *Nudge:* A tiny mix-up or a silly thing we said is enough.

2. **Just before** — What was going on just before that funny moment?

   *Nudge:* Put us back in the place. The story can be short.

3. **The funny bit** — What part of that moment makes you laugh the most?

   *Nudge:* A face, a few words, or the bit that went wrong.

4. **Only us** — What makes that joke feel like one of ours?

   *Nudge:* It may link to something we do, say, or have been through together.

5. **Tell it again** — How would you tell that funny story to someone who was not there?

   *Nudge:* Tell it your way. We do not need to agree on every detail.

**Closing invitation:** If you like, give our funny story a name to keep.

## S03 · The trip that went sideways

**Invitation:** Revisit a day out when the plan changed.

**Topic fit:** A walk, a bus ride or a local day out counts. Choose a change of plan that feels okay to revisit, or pick another story.

1. **The plan** — Think of a trip where our plan changed. What had we planned to do?

   *Nudge:* A small day out counts just as much as a long trip.

2. **The turn** — What happened on that trip that we did not expect?

   *Nudge:* The weather, a wrong turn, a closed door, or a change of heart.

3. **Our next move** — What did you do when our plan changed?

   *Nudge:* Think of your own first reaction. I can tell you about mine.

4. **Looking back** — How do you feel about that change of plan now?

   *Nudge:* Funny, still annoying, a bit of both. It does not need a happy ending.

5. **The story now** — What part of that trip would you tell someone about first?

   *Nudge:* It may be the problem, a good surprise, or how the day finally ended.

**Closing invitation:** If you like, keep one line from our day that went off plan.

## S04 · Our little habit

**Invitation:** Look at a small thing we do that has become part of us.

**Topic fit:** We do not need to live together. A greeting, a message or a way of saying goodbye counts.

1. **The habit** — What small thing do we do that feels like our thing?

   *Nudge:* A shared snack, a phrase, a call, or the way we say goodnight.

2. **How it began** — What do you remember about how our little habit started?

   *Nudge:* An early memory is enough. We do not need to find the very first time.

3. **The familiar bit** — What part of our little habit do you look forward to?

   *Nudge:* The thing itself, the timing, or knowing we will have a moment together.

4. **What it means** — What does that little habit mean to you?

   *Nudge:* It can be comforting, fun, useful, or simply something familiar.

5. **Its place now** — How would you like that habit to fit our life now?

   *Nudge:* Keep it, change it, or let it rest. We can feel differently about it.

**Closing invitation:** If you like, keep a small picture in words of our little habit.

## S05 · What came with you

**Invitation:** Share one small piece of growing up that is still with you.

**Before starting:** A more personal talk about growing up. Choose a part you feel okay sharing. Either of us can skip or stop.

1. **Back then** — What small habit or rule do you remember from where you grew up?

   *Nudge:* Think of meals, weekends, guests, quiet time, or how people said hello.

2. **An ordinary day** — What did that habit or rule look like on a normal day?

   *Nudge:* One scene is enough. You can leave out names or details.

3. **Your side of it** — How did that habit or rule feel to you back then?

   *Nudge:* You may have liked it, disliked it, or not thought much about it.

4. **Here today** — Where does that old habit or rule show up in your life now?

   *Nudge:* You might still do it, do the opposite, or notice it only now.

5. **Your own way** — What would you like to keep or change about that habit or rule?

   *Nudge:* There is room to keep one part and leave another behind.

**Closing invitation:** If you like, keep one thing you want me to know about that part of your life.

## S06 · When the day is done

**Invitation:** Help me picture the first few minutes after a busy day.

**Topic fit:** Work, study, care and full days at home all count. Being together can also mean a call or a message.

1. **The day ends** — Think of your last busy day. What were you doing when it finally slowed down?

   *Nudge:* Maybe you sat down, left work, closed a door, or put your phone away.

2. **The first need** — After a busy day like that, what do you want first?

   *Nudge:* Food, quiet, a shower, a chat, or a little time to do nothing.

3. **Alone or together** — When your day ends, how do you like me to be there for you?

   *Nudge:* Nearby, on the phone, listening, or giving you some space can all count.

4. **A small signal** — How could you let me know what you need after a busy day?

   *Nudge:* A few plain words are enough. Your needs can change from day to day.

5. **A welcome bit of help** — What small thing from me could make your next busy evening easier?

   *Nudge:* Think of help you would welcome, even if it is just leaving something for later.

**Closing invitation:** If you like, keep a few words that help us check in after a full day.

## S07 · I notice that

**Invitation:** Tell me about a small thing I do that matters to you.

**Topic fit:** Choose something you actually like. If nothing comes to mind, it is okay to choose another story.

1. **The small thing** — What is one small thing I do that you like?

   *Nudge:* It can be very ordinary, like how I greet you or bring you a drink.

2. **One real moment** — When did you last notice me doing that small thing?

   *Nudge:* A rough memory is fine. Tell me enough to picture the moment.

3. **Why it mattered** — What did that small thing mean to you at the time?

   *Nudge:* Perhaps it helped, made you smile, or showed I had thought of you.

4. **What you wanted to say** — What would you like me to know about that small thing I do?

   *Nudge:* It may matter more than I realise, or matter in a different way.

5. **Noticing you** — How do you like me to show that I notice the things you do?

   *Nudge:* A few words, quiet thanks, or a small act can feel different to each of us.

**Closing invitation:** If you like, keep a few words about a small thing that mattered.

## S08 · Close, in your own way

**Invitation:** Talk about touch that feels good to you, and room for space too.

**Before starting:** A more personal talk about touch. Wanting touch, wanting space and changing your mind are all welcome. Either of us can skip or stop.

1. **A welcome touch** — What kind of touch from me do you enjoy, if any?

   *Nudge:* Holding hands, a brief hug, or sitting close. No touch is fine too.

2. **When it fits** — At what times does touch from me feel welcome?

   *Nudge:* Time, place and mood can change what feels good. It is okay if touch is not welcome.

3. **When space fits** — At what times would you rather have some space from touch?

   *Nudge:* You do not have to defend wanting space. It is okay if no example comes to mind.

4. **Asking first** — How would you like me to ask before I touch you?

   *Nudge:* Use words that feel natural to you. Either of us can say no or change our mind.

5. **What to remember** — What would you like me to remember about touch with you?

   *Nudge:* This can be a wish, a limit, or something that changes from day to day.

**Closing invitation:** If you like, keep one thing you want me to know about touch. We can leave it here too.

## S09 · The small thing we get stuck on

**Invitation:** Take one small clash and look at what was going on for each of us.

**Before starting:** A direct talk about a small, everyday clash, like being late or choosing food. Choose one we both feel okay revisiting. Either of us can skip or stop.

**Topic fit:** This draft is for a low-stakes disagreement. It does not try to resolve big hurts or make either person accept blame. If a small example does not fit, choose another story.

1. **The small clash** — What small thing do we keep getting stuck on?

   *Nudge:* Pick one everyday example. We do not need to list everything that has gone wrong.

2. **Your side of it** — Think of the last time that came up. What mattered to you then?

   *Nudge:* Talk about your own experience. I can share mine too.

3. **The part I missed** — What do you wish I had understood in that moment?

   *Nudge:* It might be a worry, a need, or what the rest of your day had been like.

4. **A way to pause** — What could we say if that talk starts to feel tense?

   *Nudge:* A plain request for a break can be enough. We can choose a time to return to it.

5. **A possible next time** — What is one small thing we could try when that issue comes up again?

   *Nudge:* Offer an idea. We do not have to settle it or make a promise tonight.

**Closing invitation:** If you like, keep one thing you want me to understand. We can leave the issue open.

## S10 · One small money choice

**Invitation:** Start with a small choice about money and find out what sits behind it.

**Before starting:** A direct talk about what money means to each of us. We do not need to share amounts or agree on a budget. Either of us can skip or stop.

**Topic fit:** Each person can choose their own example. No shared account, spare money or spending plan is assumed. These are conversation prompts, not financial guidance.

1. **A recent choice** — What small money choice have you made lately?

   *Nudge:* Something you bought, saved for, went without, or chose to wait on.

2. **What it meant** — Why did that money choice matter to you?

   *Nudge:* Think of what you wanted, needed, enjoyed, or hoped to avoid.

3. **An old rule** — What rule about money did you grow up with?

   *Nudge:* Something people said or did. You can share only the part you feel okay sharing.

4. **What still fits** — Which part of that old money rule still fits your life?

   *Nudge:* Some, all, or none of it may fit. You can have your own way now.

5. **Talking now** — How would you like us to talk about a money choice like this?

   *Nudge:* Think of a time, a tone, or a way to ask that would help you feel heard.

**Closing invitation:** If you like, keep one thing you want me to understand about money. No budget needed tonight.

## S11 · A Saturday just for us

**Invitation:** Make up a day together, from the first slow hour to the last goodnight.

**Topic fit:** Imagine a free day, whatever day of the week works for us. Staying in and spending nothing count.

1. **The morning** — If we had a whole Saturday free, how would you like to start it with me?

   *Nudge:* Sleeping late, breakfast, a walk, or doing very little all count.

2. **The day opens up** — Where would you like our free Saturday to take us next?

   *Nudge:* Somewhere close, somewhere new, or another room at home.

3. **A silly turn** — What silly thing could we add to our Saturday just for fun?

   *Nudge:* A bad film, a made-up game, or a snack we have never tried.

4. **The evening** — How would you like our free Saturday to end?

   *Nudge:* Picture the place and what we would be doing as the day slows down.

5. **A piece for real** — Which small part of our made-up Saturday would you like to try?

   *Nudge:* One piece can be enough. We do not need a whole free day.

**Closing invitation:** If you like, keep one scene from our made-up Saturday.

## S12 · Our first try

**Invitation:** Picture us trying something new, with room to be bad at it.

**Topic fit:** Choose an idea we both feel okay exploring. A new food, game or short walk can count.

1. **The idea** — What new thing would you like to try with me?

   *Nudge:* It can be close to home, free, and small enough to try once.

2. **The first few minutes** — Picture us trying that new thing. What part would you look forward to?

   *Nudge:* The thing itself, a change of scene, or seeing a new side of me.

3. **Being bad at it** — If our first try went badly, what could make it fun anyway?

   *Nudge:* We can laugh, slow down, change the idea, or stop.

4. **Welcome help** — When you try something new, what kind of help do you like from me?

   *Nudge:* A tip, a cheer, company, or room to work it out yourself.

5. **A small version** — What would a small first try of our idea look like?

   *Nudge:* Make it easy to try and easy to leave. It does not have to become a hobby.

**Closing invitation:** If you like, keep our idea for a first try. It can stay an idea.

## S13 · A dream with no due date

**Invitation:** Tell me about a wish that is yours, without having to make it a plan.

**Before starting:** A more personal talk about something you hope for. Our wishes can be different. Either of us can skip or stop.

1. **The wish** — What is something you would love to do one day?

   *Nudge:* A small wish counts. It does not have to be something we do together.

2. **Inside the dream** — Picture a day when that dream is real. What are you doing?

   *Nudge:* Start with one ordinary detail: where you are, what you see, or how your day begins.

3. **What it offers** — What do you hope that dream would add to your life?

   *Nudge:* More fun, rest, freedom, time to learn, or something that is hard to name.

4. **My part** — What would you like from me while you think about that dream?

   *Nudge:* Listening, interest, practical help, or letting the idea rest can all count.

5. **Only if you want** — What small step, if any, would you like to take toward that dream?

   *Nudge:* Looking something up, talking more, or doing nothing for now are all possible.

**Closing invitation:** If you like, keep a few words about the dream. We can leave the date blank.

## S14 · Time to be me

**Invitation:** Talk about time for a part of you that is your own.

**Before starting:** A more personal talk about having time for yourself. We may want different amounts of time alone. Either of us can skip or stop.

1. **Something of your own** — What do you enjoy doing on your own these days?

   *Nudge:* It can be something you miss doing, from reading to seeing a friend without me.

2. **How it feels** — What do you like about having that time for yourself?

   *Nudge:* Think of how you feel during it or after it. Missing it can be part of your answer.

3. **What gets in the way** — What tends to get in the way of time for yourself?

   *Nudge:* A full day, care for others, cost, tiredness, or finding it hard to ask.

4. **What I should know** — What would you like me to know about your need for time on your own?

   *Nudge:* Explain what it means to you. We can have different needs.

5. **A little room** — What bit of time would you like for yourself in the days ahead?

   *Nudge:* A few minutes can count. This is a wish to share, not a promise to make tonight.

**Closing invitation:** If you like, keep a few words about a part of you that needs some room.

## S15 · A change we can see coming

**Invitation:** Look at one change ahead and how we can be there for each other.

**Before starting:** A more personal talk about change. We do not need to know how things will turn out. Either of us can skip or stop.

**Topic fit:** Choose a change you feel okay talking about, such as new work, a move or a busier season. If no change fits, choose another story.

1. **The change** — What change in your life has been on your mind lately?

   *Nudge:* Something definite or something that might happen can both count.

2. **A day after it** — What might a normal day look like after that change?

   *Nudge:* Picture one part of the day. You do not need to predict it all.

3. **What to keep** — As life changes, what would you like us to keep making time for?

   *Nudge:* A call, a shared meal, a joke, or a quiet moment can be enough.

4. **The unknown part** — What feels hard to know about that change right now?

   *Nudge:* It is okay to have mixed feelings or no clear answer.

5. **Being there** — What kind of support from me would feel good as things change?

   *Nudge:* Listening, checking in, practical help, or giving you room to work things out.

**Closing invitation:** If you like, keep one way we can be there for each other. The rest can stay open.

## Editorial review and a first pilot

Suggested first six to try: S01, S02, S06, S09, S11 and S13. They sample memory, humor, everyday support, a small disagreement, shared play and a personal hope. This is a proposal for this draft, not a change to the handoff's six-topic implementation pilot.

Ask readers which wording they had to read twice, which question led to a real example, where the next question felt like a jump, and how the ending felt. Do not require them to share their actual answers. Revise from consented feedback; do not infer connection from opening all five screens.

Authoring review is not a tested reading level or proof that a conversation helps a relationship. The initial mechanical check counts questions, nudges and endings and checks main-question length. Plain-word and read-aloud review still need human readers. English is the drafting language for this request; Dutch translation and emotional-equivalence review remain to do.

The handoff (§63) requires a qualified reviewer to assess sensitive later topics before publication. Apply that review to S05, S08, S09 and S10 in particular, and review the remaining Personal topics for assumptions and pressure to disclose. This does not delay Nick's authorized drafting work.

Before using any proposal in the app, decide whether it revises an existing topic or adds a distinct one. Preserve the exact content of active sessions and saved question snapshots. New subjects need their own identities, metadata, Dutch text, reviewed art/chapter mappings and an explicit place in the offer; they cannot silently inherit unrelated topic IDs or Quick eligibility. The existing 3-free / 12-paid catalogue and A06/B06/C06 exclusions remain the runtime contract until deliberately revised.
